Case 01

Fast Fourier Transform in zk-STARK Protocols: Interpolation, Encoding and Proximity Proofs

I reconstructed zk-STARKs from first principles before organizing the protocol, its optimizations and proofs.

I started from a precise difficulty: the literature uses non-uniform AIR formalisms and rarely reconstructs the entire path. Building on and expanding ideas already presented more intuitively in the literature, I formalized the transition from finite-state machines to Computational Integrity and AIR and proved their logical equivalence. On this basis, I proposed a general definition that unifies formulations from the literature and a two-preorder framework for comparing and composing valid or probabilistically sound extensions.

I applied the framework to systematically analyze and reinterpret known optimizations. In particular, the thesis formalizes permutation arguments from the literature as transformations that use auxiliary columns and a running-product argument to convert non-local constraints indexed by a permutation into constraints that are local with respect to the trace shift, with probabilistic soundness. Padding, unrolling, re-aggregation and preprocessing can then be classified and composed into a zk-VM.

Drawing on the literature, I finally reconstructed the protocol through FFT, Reed–Solomon encoding, DEEP and FRI, with complexity and soundness analyses that distinguish proven results, assumptions and limitations.

Case 02

Reversible 2D geospatial component

Hilbert-based 2D → 1D mapping with controlled discretization.

I proposed a Hilbert-based 2D → 1D mapping that preserves spatial locality and verified the round trip end to end. Increasing the Hilbert order can reduce discretization below a prescribed tolerance; on the resulting finite grid, point ↔ index conversion is exact.

I also contributed to the Python implementation using the hilbertcurve library with targeted ChatGPT assistance. I proposed Huffman coding for brightness values and contributed to the triangulation idea, without implementing those parts myself.

Case 03

OMEN ACPI Toolkit

From an incomplete Linux shutdown to the power-off path already present in the firmware.

On my HP OMEN MAX 16-ap0006sl, I isolated an S5 shutdown that left the dGPU powered. ACPI tables and logs showed that the power-off path was already present in the firmware: the override invokes PEGP.OMPR = 3 and PEGP._PS3() from _PTS(5).

The toolkit builds and verifies DSDT overrides separately from the stock entries, with fail-closed checks and recovery. Codex made substantial contributions to the Bash/Python implementation, automated tests and documentation; I defined objectives and constraints, reviewed and integrated the changes, and validated the result on the reference hardware.

  1. ProblemdGPU powered after S5
  2. AnalysisACPI tables + logs
  3. Firmware pathOMPR = 3 → _PS3()
  4. MethodVerified DSDT override
  5. SafetyStock + recovery

v2.4.0 · 14 August 2026. Physical validation: HP OMEN MAX 16-ap0006sl, board 8E35, BIOS F.13. No other hardware is claimed as validated; this is an independent project unaffiliated with HP.

Mathematics, interactive systems and C software.

Two distinct phases, presented separately.

The cartridges were created and published on RIVES in 2024. The public repositories from 2026 maintain the available post-jam material: they are not snapshots of the original source, but documented, tested and released versions.

Between October and December, I conceived and developed Bomb Flip and Slither Slide in C. Cursor mainly assisted with Bomb Flip’s animations and, to a greater extent, with Slither Slide’s implementation. Slither Slide placed 2nd out of 7 at RIVES Jam #3.

I organized provenance, mathematical documentation, tests and CI, publishing v1.0.0 releases with .sqfs cartridges and checksums. Codex assisted with refactoring, tests and documentation; I reviewed, integrated and validated the changes.

Bomb Flip

Inspired by Voltorb Flip, it is not a clone: I designed additive scoring, a countdown, scanners, Fold, twelve levels and 6 × 6 grids, and wrote most of the original gameplay. The documentation distinguishes the inherited matrix and row-or-column clue model from the cartridge’s own design choices.

B-spline · Technical View · v1.0.0

Slither Slide

I designed the mechanics and gameplay, selected a closed uniform cubic B-spline for the profile, and handled integration and validation. Movement and collision remain on a grid and a geometric chain separate from the rendered surface.

The Technical View, included in the normal build, shows the joints, full control polygon, four-control window for the selected span, samples, C_i(t) point and basis weights live; spans can be browsed without changing gameplay.

03 / Method

First the structure of the problem. Then the solution.

I do not start from the available tool. I reconstruct the problem, compare alternatives and choose what to implement only when constraints, correctness criteria and limitations are clear. When useful, I structure AI-assisted engineering workflows within the same method: I decompose tasks, define constraints and acceptance criteria, coordinate tools or agents, review their output, check regressions and retain final responsibility for validation.

  1. 01Reconstruct

    I define objects, assumptions, invariants and what must be explained.

  2. 02Compare

    I relate methods, assumptions, costs and opportunities for refinement.

  3. 03Translate

    I translate the structure into a protocol, algorithm, game or diagnostic plan.

  4. 04Verify

    I use proofs, invariants, round-trip checks, tests or hardware and state the remaining limitations.
